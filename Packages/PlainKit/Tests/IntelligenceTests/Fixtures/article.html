How tides work

By A. Mariner

Tides rise and fall twice a day on most coasts because the Moon pulls on the oceans. This paragraph, number 0, adds detail on how sailors and swimmers plan around the water.

The Sun pulls too, and when the two line up the tides are larger; these are spring tides. This paragraph, number 1, adds detail on how sailors and swimmers plan around the water.

When the Sun and the Moon pull at right angles, the range is smaller; these are neap tides. This paragraph, number 2, adds detail on how sailors and swimmers plan around the water.

The shape of a bay can make the range far larger, as in the Bay of Fundy. This paragraph, number 3, adds detail on how sailors and swimmers plan around the water.

Tide tables are made from long records at each harbor and predicted years ahead. This paragraph, number 4, adds detail on how sailors and swimmers plan around the water.
