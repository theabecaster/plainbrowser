Lighthouses of the North Sea

From the encyclopedia · 12 minute read

The North Sea coast has some of the oldest working lighthouses in Europe. Their keepers kept logs of every storm, and many of those books survive in harbor archives.

Each light has its own character: a pattern of flashes that tells a ship which tower it sees. Sailors learned them by heart.

The light is the only thing that does not move when everything else does.

Most lights were automated in the late twentieth century. The towers remain, and their lamps still turn every night.
